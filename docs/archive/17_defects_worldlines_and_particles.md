**Status:** Empirically grounded

## 1. Defects

### Definition — Defect Event

A **defect** occurs at time \( t \) if hierarchical closure changes discontinuously:
\[
|\Delta \Omega(t)| > \varepsilon
\]

All observed defects satisfy this criterion.
No defect is observed without a corresponding \( \Delta \Omega \).

---

## 2. Defect Charge

Defect charge is defined operationally as:
\[
\Delta Q := \Delta \Omega
\]

Charge is **not conserved exactly**.

---

## 3. Defect Regulation

Defects occur preferentially during periods of geometric instability
(high variance in Ω) and suppress further instability.

Defects are **regulated instabilities**, not noise.

---

## 4. Persistence and Worldlines

### Definition — Persistent Defect

A defect is persistent if successive defect events:
- occur within bounded temporal separation
- share correlated rewrite support
- exhibit bounded charge variation

---

### Definition — Worldline

A **worldline** is an equivalence class of persistent defect events
satisfying temporal and structural continuity.

Worldlines are:
- not paths in space
- not embedded trajectories
- purely relational and historical

---

## 5. Particle Definition (Operational)

### Particle Definition Theorem (HCSN)

A defect worldline constitutes a **particle** iff all of the following hold:

1. **Persistence**  
   Lifetime ≫ local rewrite correlation time

2. **Momentum Coherence**  
   Rewrite imbalance exhibits bounded variance

3. **Inertial Stability**  
   Lifetime scales inversely with momentum variance

4. **Geometric Coupling**  
   Stability correlates with hierarchical closure Ω

## 6. Consequences

- Particles are emergent processes, not objects
- Identity is defined by continuity, not constituents
- No Hilbert space or wavefunction is assumed

Particles are *what the network remembers*.
